Looking for a rehab app? Home practice to use alongside your rehab

Bonet is a home practice app. People use it alongside their rehab or therapy, between sessions, to keep up a daily routine of movement exercises counted by the camera, speaking practice with hints and short brain games. It is not a medical device, it does not treat anything, and it does not replace your therapist.

Start free

Is Bonet a rehab app?

Not in the medical sense. Bonet is a home practice app that you use alongside rehab. It is not a medical device and not a treatment. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition, it has not been tested in a clinical trial, and no regulator has reviewed or cleared it.

What you need to do for your rehab is for your rehab team to decide. Bonet gives you one simple place to practise at home between sessions.

What can I practise at home between therapy sessions?

One daily plan covers three kinds of practice. You choose what to do and how much.

  • Movement: 34 exercises for shoulders and arms, hands, sit-to-stand, legs and balance. A 3D guide shows the move and your camera counts each repetition.
  • Speaking: say words, name pictures, build sentences and listen. If you get stuck, hints grow one step at a time.
  • Thinking: six short games for memory, attention and quick thinking. The difficulty follows how you do.

Ask your therapist which exercises suit you; stop if anything hurts.

How does the camera count my exercises?

Put your phone, tablet or computer where the camera can see you. A model on your device finds body and hand positions in the picture, and simple rules count each repetition and time the set. After a set you get a short tip, for example about height, steadiness, or left and right.

  • Camera video is read on your device. It is never recorded or uploaded.
  • The count is an estimate. Light, camera angle and loose clothing can change it.
  • Do not rely on the camera to keep you safe. It can miss a loss of balance.

Can my therapist see what I practised?

Bonet makes a one-page report of the days, minutes and exercises you practised. You choose whether to print it or share it yourself; Bonet does not send it to anyone, and there is no therapist account. It is a summary of home practice, not a medical record or a clinical assessment. See an example report, or for therapists and carers.

What does it cost?

You can start free. The free plan includes 8 of the 34 exercises, speaking levels 1 to 3 and brain games on Easy. Premium is US$9.99 a month, with 7 days free, and you can cancel any time.

Is it safe?

Ask your therapist which exercises suit you; stop if anything hurts.

Ask your doctor or therapist first, especially if you have had a stroke, a heart problem, a recent fall or balance problems. Start slow, use a clear space with good light, and stop if you feel pain, dizziness or unwell. Read what to know before you start.

If you notice sudden face drooping, weakness or trouble speaking, call your local emergency number right away. Bonet cannot call for help.
