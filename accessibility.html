Accessibility statement

Bonet is made for older adults, so it should be easy to see, read and use. We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA. We do not claim that the website or the app fully meets them yet. This page says what we have checked and what we have not.

Start free

What have we checked?

On 14 November 2026 we ran an automated accessibility check (axe-core) on the home page and the pricing page of this website, in several of its languages, at computer and phone screen sizes.

We fixed what it found: buttons with too little colour contrast, headings out of order, buttons whose spoken name did not match their visible text, and links too small to tap easily. After the fixes, the tool reported no problems on those pages.

Automated tools find only some kinds of problems. A clean result does not mean a page is fully accessible.

What helps on this website?

  • An A+ button at the top of every page makes the text bigger.
  • A skip link lets keyboard users jump straight to the main content.
  • If your device is set to reduce motion, most animations stop.
  • Animated previews have a written description for screen readers.
  • Every page is in eight languages.

What helps in the app?

  • Simple mode: one big Start button instead of a busy screen.
  • A large text setting.
  • Eight languages, for the app and the speaking practice.

What have we not tested yet?

  • The other pages of this website.
  • The app at app.bonet.care.
  • Use with screen readers, switch access or voice control by the people who rely on them.

Some parts of practice depend on the device. Exercise counting needs the camera to see you, and some speech recognition needs an internet connection. Counts and speech results are estimates and can be wrong.

How do I tell you about a problem?

Email hello@coglixlabs.com. Tell us the page or screen, your device and browser, and any assistive technology you use. We will use what you tell us to decide what to fix next.
