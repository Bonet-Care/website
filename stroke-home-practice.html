A stroke app for daily home practice

Bonet is an app for daily practice at home, used by older adults and by people practising after a stroke. It puts movement exercises counted by your camera, speaking practice with hints and short brain games into one daily plan. It is a practice tool, not a medical device, and it does not replace your therapist.

Start free

What can I practise at home after a stroke with Bonet?

One daily plan covers three kinds of practice. You choose what to do and how much.

  • Movement: 34 exercises for shoulders and arms, hands, sit-to-stand, legs and balance. A 3D guide shows the move and your camera counts each repetition.
  • Speaking: say words, name pictures, build sentences and listen. If you get stuck, hints grow one step at a time.
  • Thinking: six short games for memory, attention and quick thinking. The difficulty follows how you do.

What does Bonet do, and what does it not do?

What it does:

  • It counts repetitions, times each set and gives a short tip after a session.
  • In speaking practice it shows the words it recognised, your pace and your pauses.
  • It keeps a record of the days and minutes you practised, and a one-page report you can print or share.

What it does not do:

  • It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition.
  • It does not replace your doctor, therapist or speech-language pathologist.
  • It has not been tested in a clinical trial, and no regulator has reviewed or cleared it.
  • Its numbers are estimates from a camera and a microphone. They can be wrong.

How do I start?

  1. Ask your doctor or therapist first, and show them the exercises you plan to do.
  2. Open app.bonet.care in a browser on a phone, tablet or computer with a camera and a microphone.
  3. Answer a few setup questions. Bonet suggests a short plan for today.
  4. Sit in a stable chair, or stand next to something sturdy you can hold, and begin with a few minutes.

The free plan is enough to try all three kinds of practice. Premium is US$9.99 a month, with 7 days free.

When should I ask my therapist?

  • Before you start, and whenever the app and your therapist say different things. Do what your therapist says.
  • If an exercise hurts, feels too hard, or you are not sure it is safe for you.
  • If you want to know what your numbers mean. Bonet shows what you practised, not how you are doing.

Stop if you feel pain, dizziness or unwell. If you notice sudden face drooping, weakness or trouble speaking, call your local emergency number right away. Bonet cannot call for help.

Can I share my practice with my therapist or family?

Yes. Bonet makes a one-page report of the days, minutes and exercises you practised. You print it or share it yourself; Bonet does not send it to anyone. It is a summary of home practice, not a medical record or a clinical assessment. See an example report.
