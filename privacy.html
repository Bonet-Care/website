Bonet Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

The short version

  • Bonet is a home practice app made by Coglix Labs Inc. We are a US company. Your data is processed in the United States.
  • Your camera video is processed on your device. It is never uploaded or saved. We do not save your voice recordings either.
  • We save the results of your practice: which exercise or game, when, how long, scores, and speaking measures such as pace and pauses.
  • We do not sell your data. We do not use it for advertising. We have no advertising pixels.
  • You can delete your account and your practice history yourself in Settings. This works right away.
  • Bonet is not a medical device. We are not a hospital or a clinic. The US health privacy law called HIPAA generally does not apply to a consumer app like this one. Other privacy laws do, and we follow them.
  • If something goes wrong with your data, we will tell you.
  • If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, a section below gives you your rights there.

Questions: privacy@coglixlabs.com

Who we are

Coglix Labs Inc. ("Coglix", "we", "us") is a Delaware corporation. We make Bonet, including the website at bonet.care, the web app at app.bonet.care, and the Bonet apps for iPhone and iPad. For privacy questions we are the company that decides how your data is used.

To reach us, write to privacy@coglixlabs.com. We do not publish a postal address; please contact us by email.

If you are looking for health data rights in Washington, Nevada or other states, read the separate Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy. A copy of it is also at the end of this page and is part of this policy.

Some of this information is about your health or your abilities. We treat all of it as sensitive.

What we never collect or store

  • Camera video or images. The camera feed is analyzed on your device to find body and hand positions. The video is not recorded, saved or uploaded.
  • Raw audio recordings of your voice. We do not save them. See "Speech" for where audio may be processed while you are speaking.
  • Your card number. Stripe or Apple handles payment.
  • Your precise location. We do not ask for it. Our service providers see the network address (IP address) of your connection, from which an approximate region can be worked out.
  • Contacts, photos, or files on your device.

How we use your data

  • To run Bonet: show your history, plan your practice, count your repetitions, run the league, and produce the report you can print or share.
  • To manage your account and subscription, including sign-in codes and messages about your account.
  • To keep Bonet secure, prevent abuse and fix problems.
  • To understand, in a general way, how the product is used so we can improve it.
  • To follow the law and protect our rights.

We do not use your data to train artificial intelligence models. We do not use your health-related data for advertising. We do not sell it.

Where US law asks for your consent (for example, in Washington for health data), we rely on the choices you make in Bonet and you can withdraw them. See "Your choices and rights".

On-device processing

Some of the work Bonet does happens on your phone, tablet or computer and the raw material never leaves it:

  • Pose estimation. Your camera frames are analyzed on your device to find joints and hands. Only the numbers that come out of that, such as a repetition count, are saved.
  • Speech recognition, where your device supports it. On iPhone and iPad, Apple's speech recognition runs on the device if it supports your language. On the web, English can be recognized on the device.
  • Your daily plan. A set of rules on your device picks your plan. It is not generative AI.

If a step cannot run on your device, we say so below.

Speech

The words you say are turned into text so Bonet can compare them with the practice prompt and measure pace and pauses. We do not keep the audio. We keep the text and the measures.

Where you use Bonet Language What happens
iPhone or iPad app All supported languages Apple speech recognition. On the device if the device supports the language. Otherwise audio is sent to Apple under Apple's own privacy terms
Web, English English On-device recognition first. If it is not ready, audio may be sent through our server to Google Cloud Speech-to-Text, or your browser's own speech recognition may be used
Web, other languages Cantonese, Mandarin (Traditional and Simplified), Spanish, Japanese Audio is sent through our server to Google Cloud Speech-to-Text to be turned into text. Your browser's own speech recognition may be used as a backup, and it sends audio to the browser maker

Audio sent through our server is passed to Google and is not stored by us. What Google or your browser maker does with audio it receives is governed by their own terms. We ask for your permission before the microphone is used the first time. If you do not want audio to leave your device, you can choose not to use speaking practice, or deny microphone access in your device or browser settings. The camera exercises and brain games work without the microphone.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell personal information.
  • We do not share personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising ("targeted ads"). We use no advertising pixels or advertising software development kits.
  • We do not train AI models on your data.
  • We do not build voiceprints or face templates, and we do not identify you from your voice or body.

Cookies and analytics

The Cookie Policy lists every item we store on your device, what it does and how long it lasts. In short:

  • Bonet stores a few things that it needs to work: your sign-in, your settings, and a finished practice session until it uploads.
  • The website (bonet.care) uses Google Analytics, which sets cookies. The app uses PostHog for product analytics, tied to a random account number and without session recording.
  • On the website in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google Analytics stays off until you choose "Accept".
  • On the website elsewhere, Google Analytics is on, and you can switch it off at any time with "Cookie settings" at the bottom of every page. We honor the Global Privacy Control signal and Do Not Track on the website.
  • In the app, analytics is on unless you switch it off in Settings > Privacy. If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track and you have made no choice, the app's analytics stays off.
  • Bonet uses no advertising cookies and no advertising tools.

Where it is stored and how we protect it

  • Data moves between your device and our servers over an encrypted connection (TLS).
  • Our database uses row-level access rules, so each account can read and change only its own data.
  • Bonet is not end-to-end encrypted. We and our hosting provider can technically access stored data.
  • We do not hold a security or privacy certification, and we have not been audited against HIPAA, SOC 2, ISO 27001 or a similar standard.

No system is perfectly secure. Use a strong password and do not share it. Sign out on shared devices.

If there is a breach

If your personal information is accessed or disclosed without your authorization, including a disclosure to a third party that you did not agree to, we will tell you without unreasonable delay and no later than the time the law requires. We follow the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and state breach laws. For the FTC rule that means notice to you and, where required, to the FTC and the media within 60 days of discovery. The notice will say what happened, what data was involved, what we are doing, and what you can do. If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, we also tell the data protection authority within 72 hours of becoming aware, where that rule applies to us, and we tell you without undue delay when the risk to you is high.

Your choices and rights

You can:

  • See and correct your information in the app, or ask us for a copy.
  • Delete your account and practice history in Settings. You can also email us.
  • Download your practice history from the app (Settings, Download my data). For other data, email us and we will send it in a common format.
  • Turn off analytics in the app (Settings > Privacy), microphone and camera access in your device settings, and reminders in Settings.
  • Leave the league at any time. This deletes your nickname and league results.
  • Withdraw consent where we relied on it.
  • Opt out of sale and sharing for advertising. We do not do either, so there is nothing to opt out of. If that changes, we will tell you first and give you a clear opt-out.
  • Limit use of sensitive information. We use sensitive information only to give you the service you asked for.

Depending on where you live (for example California, Washington, Nevada, Connecticut, Colorado, Virginia and Texas), you may have these rights by law. We give the same rights to everyone in the United States.

How to ask: email privacy@coglixlabs.com from the address on your account. We may ask you to confirm it is you. We answer within 45 days (one month if you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland; see the section for those regions). If we need more time we will tell you why. You will not be treated worse for using your rights.

If we say no or you disagree: reply to our answer and write "Privacy Appeal". A person who was not involved in the first decision will review it and answer in writing within 45 days. If you are still not satisfied, you can complain to your state attorney general, for example the Washington Attorney General's office at atg.wa.gov, the Nevada Attorney General, or the California Privacy Protection Agency.

Authorized agent: you can ask someone to act for you. We may need proof.

Children

Bonet is for adults. It is not for children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect information from them. If you think a child has given us information, email us and we will delete it. When you set up Bonet it asks for an age band, which you can skip.

HIPAA

HIPAA is a US law that covers health care providers, health plans and the companies that work for them. When you use Bonet on your own, we are not one of those, so HIPAA generally does not apply to your data. Other laws protect it, and we follow them. If we ever offer Bonet through a clinic or a health plan, we will update this policy first.

International users

Bonet is operated from the United States, and we process your information there. If you use Bonet from another country, your information is transferred to and stored in the United States, where privacy law may differ from yours. The next section gives extra rights and information to people in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland. Other places have their own rules; if you are in one, write to us and we will tell you how to use your rights.

Health information and your explicit consent. Some of what you do in Bonet says something about your health or abilities: your setup answers (for example which side is weaker), exercise results, speaking measures and brain game results. The law calls this "data concerning health" and treats it as special. We process it only to give you the service you asked for, and we rely on your explicit consent, which you give when you choose to answer the setup questions and use these features. You can skip questions, and you can use parts of Bonet without them. You can withdraw your consent at any time by deleting your account in Settings or by writing to us. Withdrawing does not make earlier use unlawful. We do not use this information for advertising, we do not sell it, and we do not use it to train AI models.

Analytics and cookies. On the website we rely on your consent, which you give or refuse in the cookie notice. See the Cookie Policy.

No decisions made only by a machine. Bonet's daily plan and practice scores are suggestions and measures. They do not decide anything about your rights, your access to services, credit, insurance, work or medical care, and you can ignore or change them. We do not make decisions that have legal or similarly significant effects on you using only automated processing.

Where your information goes. Your information is processed in the United States by the providers listed in "Who processes data for us". The United States is outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland. Where the law requires a safeguard for this transfer, we rely on the safeguards our providers offer, such as the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (with its UK and Swiss extensions) or the standard contractual clauses approved for that purpose. Write to us if you want to know which applies to a provider.

How long we keep it. As in "How long we keep data".

Your rights. You have the right to:

  • ask for access to your information and a copy of it;
  • ask us to correct it;
  • ask us to erase it (Settings, Delete my account);
  • ask us to restrict how we use it;
  • object to uses based on our legitimate interests;
  • receive the information you gave us in a common, machine-readable format (portability; Settings, Download my data);
  • withdraw consent at any time;
  • not be subject to a decision based only on automated processing that has legal or similarly significant effects (we make none).

Write to privacy@coglixlabs.com. We answer within one month. If your request is complex or we receive many, we can extend that by up to two more months, and we tell you why within the first month. We do not charge a fee unless a request is clearly unfounded or excessive. We may need to confirm that you are the person concerned.

Complaints. You have the right to complain to a data protection authority, in the country where you live, work or where you think the law was broken. In the UK this is the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk). In Switzerland it is the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner. In the EU you can find your authority on the European Data Protection Board's website (edpb.europa.eu). We would like the chance to help first, but you do not have to contact us before you complain.

Children. Bonet is for adults. If you are setting it up for someone else, they must agree to the use of their information.

Mandatory rights. Nothing in this policy takes away rights that the law of your country gives you and that cannot be waived.

Changes

If we change this policy, we will change the date at the top. If the change matters, such as a new type of data, a new purpose or a new kind of sharing, we will tell you in the app or by email before it takes effect, and where the law requires it we will ask for your consent first. Older versions are available on request.

It adds to the rest of this policy and is meant to be read with it. A separate copy of this notice is on its own page at bonet.care/consumer-health-data.
