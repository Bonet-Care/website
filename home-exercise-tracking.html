How to know if home exercises were done

Bonet counts each repetition with the camera on the person's own device and records the days, minutes and exercises practised. The person can print or share a one-page report, for example at the next therapy visit. The counts are estimates, and Bonet is a practice tool, not a medical device.

What does Bonet record during home practice?

  • The days practised, the minutes and the current streak.
  • The exercises done, with sessions and repetitions.
  • Speaking: tries, words recognised, pace and pauses.
  • Brain games played and items right.

Camera video is analysed on the device and never recorded or uploaded. Voice recordings are not kept.

How does the camera count a repetition?

A repetition is counted when you reach the position, hold it for about one second, and then come back to the start. The next repetition begins after you have returned.

The count is an estimate. Light, camera angle and loose clothing can make it wrong. See how camera rep counting works.

How does a therapist or family member see the results?

  • The person prints the one-page report or shares it, for example at the next visit.
  • The report is shared only when the person prints it or shares it. Bonet does not send it to family or therapists.
  • There are no dashboards, remote monitoring or alerts in the app today.

The report shows what was practised. It is not a medical record or a clinical assessment. See an example report.

Does a therapist need an account or pay per patient?

No. There is no therapist account and no fee per patient. Each person uses their own Bonet account, on the free plan or on Premium. A therapist only looks at the report the person chooses to share.

How do I get someone started?

  1. Make sure the person agrees to use Bonet and has checked with their doctor or therapist.
  2. Open app.bonet.care on their phone, tablet or computer. When the app asks who will use it, say you are setting it up for someone else.
  3. Answer the setup questions together. Turn on Simple mode and large text if a calmer screen helps.
  4. Clear a safe space with good light and a stable chair, and stay near for balance exercises.

What does Bonet not do?

  • It is not a medical device and not a therapy. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition.
  • It does not replace a doctor, therapist or speech-language pathologist.
  • Its numbers are estimates from a camera and a microphone. They can be wrong.

What does it cost?

You can start free. The free plan includes 8 of the 34 exercises, speaking levels 1 to 3 and brain games on Easy. Premium is US$9.99 a month, with 7 days free, and you can cancel any time.
