How Bonet's automatic measures work, and their limits

Last updated: 13 November 2026

The short version

  • Bonet uses automatic tools to count your repetitions, listen for the words you say, and choose a daily plan. It does not use a chatbot, and it does not talk to you using generative AI.
  • It does not diagnose anything. It does not make medical decisions.
  • The numbers are estimates. They can be wrong, and they are for practice, not for medical use.
  • Your camera video stays on your device. We do not save your voice recordings. We do not use your data to train AI models.
  • If you feel pain or dizziness, stop. In an emergency, call 911.

Bonet does not include: a chatbot, generative AI that writes advice, a diagnosis tool, a risk score, a recovery prediction, or any tool that decides on treatment. Pose estimation and speech recognition use machine-learning models made by other companies or open projects: Google's MediaPipe for body and hand positions, OpenAI's Whisper for on-device English recognition on the web, and Apple's or Google's speech recognition where your device or language needs it.

Computer players

To keep leagues lively, some players in the weekly league are computer players. They are not real people. The league page in the app says that some players are computer players.

What can go wrong

Automatic tools are not perfect. Here is when they tend to fail.

Camera and exercises

  • Dim light, strong backlight, or a busy background.
  • The camera too close, too far, too low or at a strange angle.
  • Loose or dark clothing that hides joints, or a chair that hides your legs.
  • More than one person in view.
  • Movements that are small, slow, or done in a different way than the guide shows, including after injury or stroke. The app may not count a repetition that you did well, or count one that you did not.
  • Range-of-motion numbers are estimates from a flat camera image. They are not the same as a measurement by a therapist.

Speech

  • Background noise, a poor microphone, or sitting far from the device.
  • Accents, dialects and mixing languages.
  • Slow, quiet, or unclear speech, or speech changed by a stroke or another condition. Speech recognition tools are usually built and tested on typical voices, so they may do worse with some older voices and with speech affected by a condition. The app may miss words you said well, which can make a score look low.
  • Names and uncommon words.
  • Pace and pause numbers depend on what the recognizer heard. Treat them as rough.

Scores and plan

  • A score from one day says little. A trend over weeks says more, and even then it is only a guide.
  • The daily plan does not know how you feel today or what your clinician advised. You are in charge.

What to do with the numbers

  • Use them for encouragement and for a record of what you did.
  • Do not use them to decide whether you are getting better or worse, to change your medicines or therapy, or to decide whether to see a clinician.
  • If a number surprises you, or you feel worse, tell your clinician. Do not rely on the app to tell you.
  • Carers and family: look at the trend with the person, and ask them how they feel. Do not judge them by one score.
  • Therapists: a Bonet report is a log of practice that your patient chose to share. It is not a clinical measurement and should not be used for assessment, diagnosis or treatment decisions. You decide what, if anything, to do with it.

Safety

  • Stop if you feel pain, dizziness or unwell, whatever the app says.
  • Use a stable support and have someone nearby for balance work.
  • If you think it is an emergency, including signs of a stroke, call 911. Bonet cannot call for help.
  • See What to know before you start.

People are in charge

Bonet has no human reviewing your practice in real time. Nobody at Coglix reads your results as a clinician would, and we do not step in on your health. We do look at reports of problems. You, and your clinician if you have one, decide what to do.

How your data is used here

  • Camera video: processed on your device, never uploaded or saved.
  • Voice audio: not saved by Bonet. Where your device cannot recognize speech on its own, audio goes to Apple or Google (or your browser's maker) to be turned into text, under their terms. See the Privacy Policy.
  • Training: we do not use your video, audio, recognized text or results to train or improve machine-learning models. Audio that goes to Apple or Google for recognition is handled under their terms.
  • Automatic decisions: none that have legal or similarly significant effects on you. Nothing in Bonet approves, denies or prices anything based on your health.
  • No sale, no advertising.

Tell us about a problem

If a count, score or recognition was wrong in a way that matters, or the app behaved in a way that could harm someone, email hello@coglixlabs.com. Tell us the device, the app version, what you were doing and what happened. Do not send recordings or health records by email. In an emergency, call 911 first.
