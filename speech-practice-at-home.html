Speech practice at home, with hints when you get stuck

Bonet lets you practise speaking at home, a few minutes a day. You say words, name pictures, build sentences and listen. When you get stuck, hints grow one step at a time. Each practice type has 100 levels, in 8 languages. It is a practice tool, not speech therapy, and does not diagnose or treat.

Start free

What kinds of speaking practice are there?

  • Say words and phrases aloud.
  • Name pictures.
  • Build sentences.
  • Listen.

Each type has 100 levels, so you can begin where it feels comfortable and move up slowly. Items you found hard come back later for review.

What happens when I cannot find the word?

You get a hint. Hints grow one step at a time, so you get just enough help to keep going. Using hints is part of practice, not a mistake.

What does Bonet measure when I speak?

Speech recognition turns what you say into text. Bonet compares the text with the prompt and works out:

  • the words it recognised;
  • your pace;
  • the length of your pauses;
  • a score for the try.

Recognition runs on your device where the device supports it. Otherwise it is handled by Apple (on iPhone and iPad) or Google (on the web, for several languages). Bonet keeps the recognised text and the measures. It does not keep voice recordings. See what Bonet tracks.

Why did it miss words I said well?

Speech recognition is less accurate with some accents, quiet or slow speech, background noise, and speech changed by a stroke. A low score may be the app's mistake, not yours. Move closer to the microphone, find a quiet place, or use the hints.

Which languages can I practise in?

Eight languages: English, Chinese, Japanese, Spanish, German and French.

Is this speech therapy?

No. Bonet is practice you do yourself, for example between visits. It does not assess your speech or replace a speech-language pathologist. Ask your therapist which practice suits you and how often to do it. You can bring the one-page report to your next visit.
