What to know before you start

Last updated: 13 November 2026

The short version

  • Bonet helps you practice at home. It is not a medical device. It does not diagnose or treat anything.
  • Ask your doctor or therapist before you start. Do what they say if it differs from the app.
  • Stop if you feel pain, dizziness or unwell.
  • If you think it is an emergency, call 911 or your local emergency number. Do not wait for the app.

What Bonet is, and is not

Bonet is a practice tool for exercises, speaking and brain games. It counts what you do and shows you a record.

  • It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition.
  • It does not replace your doctor, therapist or speech-language pathologist.
  • It has not been reviewed or cleared by the US Food and Drug Administration or any other regulator.
  • The numbers it shows are estimates. They can be wrong.

Before you begin

Talk to your doctor or therapist first, especially if you:

  • have had a stroke, a heart problem, or a recent injury or operation;
  • have balance problems, dizziness, or have fallen recently;
  • have pain, or limits on what movement is safe for you;
  • take medicines that make you drowsy or unsteady.

Show them what you plan to do in Bonet. They can tell you which exercises suit you and how much to do.

Stay safe while you practice

  • Use a clear space with good light. Move furniture and loose rugs out of the way.
  • Sit in a stable chair, or stand next to a wall, a counter or a sturdy table you can hold.
  • If balance is a concern, practice seated, or have someone nearby while you do balance work.
  • Wear shoes or non-slip footwear, and clothes that do not get in the way.
  • Start slow. You can skip, shorten or repeat any exercise.
  • Rest and drink water. Do not push through discomfort.
  • Do not rely on the camera to keep you safe. It can miss a bad movement or a loss of balance.

Stop and get advice if

  • you have pain, chest tightness, or trouble breathing;
  • you feel dizzy, faint, nauseous or very tired;
  • you feel new weakness, numbness or confusion, or your speech changes;
  • you fall or nearly fall.

If these come on suddenly, treat it as an emergency.

Emergencies

Call 911 (or your local emergency number) right away for:

  • face drooping, arm or leg weakness, trouble speaking or seeing, or sudden confusion (possible stroke);
  • chest pain, severe shortness of breath, a serious fall or injury.

Bonet cannot call for help for you and does not watch over you. If you are in distress or thinking of harming yourself, call or text 988 in the United States, or contact your local crisis line.

What the numbers mean

Bonet shows repetition counts, range-of-motion estimates, speaking pace, pauses and words recognized, and scores. These are for practice and encouragement.

  • Light, camera angle, clothing, your device, your accent and the way you speak can change them.
  • A low score on a day does not mean something is wrong. A high one does not mean you are well.
  • Speech recognition is less accurate for some voices, including some older voices and speech affected by a stroke. It may miss words you said well.
  • Do not use Bonet to decide about your medicines or your therapy. Ask your clinician.

Read more on how automatic measures work and their limits.

Sharing with your therapist or family

You can print or share a one-page report. It shows what you practiced. It is not a medical record, an assessment or a clinical report, and it is not a substitute for a visit. You decide who sees it. Therapists and family can use it to start a conversation.

If you are helping someone else

Carers and family: please make sure the person agrees to use Bonet, that they have checked with their clinician, that the space is safe, and that someone is near for balance work. You know them best. Stop if they seem unwell.

Leagues and computer players

The weekly league is for fun. Some players in a league are computer players, not real people. Do not feel you must keep up. Take rest days.

Report a problem

If Bonet gave you wrong information, did not work as it should, or you or someone else was hurt or nearly hurt while using it, email hello@coglixlabs.com. We will read it. In an emergency, call 911 first.

Accessibility

Bonet has a large-text option and a simple mode. If something is hard to see, hear or use, tell us at hello@coglixlabs.com.
