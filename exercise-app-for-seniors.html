Exercise app for seniors: gentle exercises at home

Bonet is a home exercise app made with older adults in mind. A 3D guide shows each gentle exercise, your camera counts the repetitions on your device, and several exercises are done seated. Big text and a Simple mode keep the screen calm. It is a practice tool, not a medical device.

Start free

What exercises are in the app?

There are 34 exercises, grouped by part of the body. The free plan includes 8 of them.

  • Shoulders and arms
  • Hands and wrists
  • Neck, back and trunk
  • Legs, sit-to-stand and balance

How does the camera count my exercises?

Put your phone, tablet or computer where the camera can see you. A model on your device finds body and hand positions in the picture, and simple rules count each repetition and time the set. After a set you get a short tip, for example about height, steadiness, or left and right.

  • Camera video is read on your device. It is never recorded or uploaded.
  • The count is an estimate. Light, camera angle and loose clothing can change it.
  • Do not rely on the camera to keep you safe. It can miss a loss of balance.

Can I exercise sitting down?

Yes. Several exercises are done seated, such as seated knee extension and seated trunk rotation, and you can skip, shorten or repeat any exercise. If balance is a concern, practise seated, or stand next to a wall or a sturdy table and have someone nearby.

Is it easy to use for older adults?

  • Big text: a large-text option makes everything easier to read.
  • Simple mode: one big Start button instead of a busy screen.
  • One daily plan, so you do not have to choose from a long list.
  • 8 languages, including English, Chinese, Japanese, Spanish, German and French.
  • A corgi coach named Bonet, a streak and a weekly league, if you like a little encouragement.

What does it cost?

You can start free. The free plan includes 8 of the 34 exercises, speaking levels 1 to 3 and brain games on Easy. Premium is US$9.99 a month, with 7 days free, and you can cancel any time.

Is it safe for me to start?

Ask your doctor or therapist first, especially if you have had a stroke, a heart problem, a recent fall or balance problems. Start slow, use a clear space with good light, and stop if you feel pain, dizziness or unwell. Read what to know before you start.
